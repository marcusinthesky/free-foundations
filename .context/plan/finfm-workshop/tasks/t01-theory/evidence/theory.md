## Result 1: representability does not identify geometry

Let histories be H, economic state s:H→S and frozen representation z:H→Z. With an unrestricted set-theoretic readout, there exists r:Z→S with s=r∘z **if and only if** z(h)=z(h′) implies s(h)=s(h′). Necessity follows by applying r; for sufficiency define r on z(H) by its unique state value and extend it arbitrarily, assuming S is nonempty. If S is empty, handle the empty-domain case separately. This is an exact, pointwise statement; with measurable readouts the fiber condition alone does not ensure a measurable extension. For conditional-expectation targets use the almost-sure measurable statement `Sufficiency.condExp_eq_iff`, under that declaration's measure and integrability assumptions.

A bijection g:Z→Z carries z to g∘z and replaces r by r∘g⁻¹, retaining s. Thus unrestricted recoverability does not identify a metric on Z. In R² take z₀=(0,0), z₁=(1,0), z₂=(0,2) and g=diag(3,1): z₁ is nearer to z₀ before the transformation, and z₂ afterward. The inverse readout recovers precisely the original state. This example shows non-identification, not failure of every particular encoder. `Geometry.sufficient_equiv`, `Neighbours.anisotropic_flip`, and `Neighbours.stretch_predictions` check the constituent facts; the fiber iff itself follows directly from `Alignment.state_optimal_iff` with F=s and state=z, provided the codomain S is nonempty. `Alignment.optimal_imp_sufficient_iff` is a different universal statement: with nonempty economic state space, every F-optimal representation is state-sufficient iff F-fibers refine state-fibers.

For a restricted readout class R⊆S^Z define its **class stabilizer** as

`G_R = {g: Z ≃ Z : {r∘g⁻¹ : r∈R} = R}`.

Equality, not one-sided inclusion, is required: it makes closure hold in both directions and makes G_R a group under composition. For g in G_R, attainable state functions `{r∘z:r∈R}` and `{r∘g∘z:r∈R}` coincide. Proof: substitute q=r∘g and use equality (equivalently its inverse). A non-isometric g within this particular stabilizer and a suitable configuration establish non-identification for that class. The unrestricted example above does not establish such a g for every restricted class; linear readouts, for example, are stabilized by invertible linear recodings, including the stretch. This stabilizer equality and its group argument are hand proofs, not a named Lean declaration.

Approximate recovery requires a declared norm, distribution and approximation class; exact recoverability is not an approximation rate. Given true exposure aᵢ, estimates âᵢ and valid simultaneous bounds ‖âⱼ−aⱼ‖≤δⱼ≤δmax, suppose the estimated k-neighbor set S excludes i and its smallest member/nonmember gap is γ>2(δᵢ+δmax). Then S is also a strict true-distance neighbor set; equal cardinality and exclusion of i identify it uniquely. Indeed each row-distance error is at most δᵢ+δmax and any member/nonmember gap can shrink by at most twice that amount. `Neighbours.neighbourSet_of_estimated_margin` proves the strict-set conclusion and `Neighbours.neighbourSet_unique` supplies uniqueness. Ties and γ equal to the threshold are not covered (`Neighbours.neighbourSet_margin_sharp`). Failure to certify a row is inconclusive about its true neighbors. Empirical margins alone do not establish δ; provide external/conditional error certificates and their simultaneous coverage, or report the gap as a descriptive sensitivity diagnostic. Exact graph equality further requires all rows' neighbor sets to be certified under identical roster and tie rules.

## Result 2: the implemented graph and spatial stability

Let K be a finite symmetric, nonnegative adjacency with positive degrees dᵢ=ΣⱼKᵢⱼ, W=D⁻¹K, and dmin≤dᵢ≤dmax. For |λ|<1, P=(I−λW)⁻¹ exists and

`‖P‖₂ ≤ sqrt(dmax/dmin)/(1−|λ|)`.

Proof: W is nonnegative and row stochastic, d is stationary by symmetry, and Jensen's inequality yields ‖Wx‖_D≤‖x‖_D. Therefore ‖(I−λW)x‖_D≥(1−|λ|)‖x‖_D, which in finite dimension proves invertibility and the weighted inverse bound. Comparing weighted and Euclidean norms yields the displayed degree factor. This is exactly the conclusion and hypotheses of `ReversibleNetwork.normalize_resolvent_l2_bound`; `normalize_nonneg`, `normalize_row_sum`, `stationary_degree`, `spatial_isUnit`, and `weighted_resolvent_bound` give its steps. W is similar to D⁻¹/²KD⁻¹/², so its eigenvalues are real in [−1,1]. The bound uses distance to |λ|=1, a sufficient stability margin, not the actual minimum distance to the reciprocal spectrum or a necessary invertibility condition. Similarity itself is a hand argument here.

The actual `study/networks.py::knn_weights` selects k nearest non-self rows with stable roster-order tie breaks, sets Kᵢⱼ=1 when either i selects j or j selects i, and row-normalizes. For 1≤k<n every dᵢ≥k, so isolates cannot occur; ties can make the chosen directed graph roster-dependent. Report the observed dmin, dmax, ratio, λ, 1−|λ| and spectrum, and check the roster and finite distances. Symmetric zero-diagonal industry affinities can contain isolates. The implementation leaves an isolated row zero; for a symmetric K the corresponding column is zero too. Permuting active vertices and isolates makes W block diagonal with an active stochastic block and a zero block, and P has an identity isolate block. Its norm is at most max{1, sqrt(dmax/dmin)/(1−|λ|)} using positive-degree vertices only; if all are isolated, P=I. This isolate corollary is a hand argument, not directly the positive-degree Lean theorem. It does not apply to arbitrary directed zero rows without further structure.

For two graphs on the same roster, same λ and innovation covariance V, suppose their inverses have common norm bound M and ‖V‖₂≤v. Then

`‖P̂−P‖₂ ≤ |λ| M² ‖Ŵ−W‖₂`,

`‖P̂ V P̂ᵀ−P V Pᵀ‖₂ ≤ 2 v |λ| M³ ‖Ŵ−W‖₂`.

Apply the first result separately to both graphs to choose M, using their own degree ratios and boundary margins. The inverse identity and covariance product expansion prove these inequalities; see `Stability.resolvent_sub_le` and `Stability.matrix_covariance_sub_le` (the latter explicitly requires both determinant units and both inverse bounds). For estimated λ and V, use `Stability.matrix_covariance_joint_sub_le`: with H=λW and Ĥ=λ̂Ŵ the right side is `2vM³‖H−Ĥ‖₂ + M²‖V−V̂‖₂`; coefficient and graph errors can be separated by `Stability.smul_network_sub_le`. For any fixed portfolio w, the variance error is at most ‖w‖₂² times the covariance bound; comparison of minimum-variance choices on a common feasible set with ‖w‖≤B incurs at most twice B² times that bound (`Stability.matrix_portfolio_regret` under its exact feasibility assumptions). Positive-semidefinite V suffices for covariance interpretation; positive eigenvalue floors are additional requirements for inverse-covariance scores. Model approximation error to actual covariance is additional.

A hard k-NN map is discontinuous at neighbor ties: no generic small distance error implies small graph error without margins. Under valid simultaneous row certificates, identical directed selections imply identical symmetrized K and W; otherwise report the observed perturbation ‖Ŵ−W‖₂ or establish a separate graph perturbation bound. Exponential-kernel bounds in `Kernel`/`Pipeline` are for their stated dense exponential construction and cannot be transferred automatically to binary k-NN. The bounds concern a fixed finite panel; uniform asymptotic claims require degree ratios, innovation norms and boundary margins controlled uniformly with N.

## Interpretation, estimation and diagnostics

A frozen encoder removes estimation of its weights on the financial sample, not estimation of preprocessing, context, feature selection, distance calibration, exposure readout, factor covariance/loadings, innovation variances or λ. List fitted parameters and effective degrees of freedom for every comparison, including bandwidth, regularization and model/graph selection; distinguish fixed checkpoint features from supervised calibration. A smaller fitted class can reduce complexity under a common sampling design, but freezing alone gives no automatic generalization, identification, valid inference or smaller error. `Selection.finite_family` gives for K predetermined candidates and B independent nonempty blocks, losses in [0,1], a uniform deviation probability at most `2|K| exp(−2Bε²)`. Within-block dependence and dependence across candidate losses are allowed, while losses for each fixed candidate must be independent across blocks. `Selection.coupled_finite_family` adds an explicitly supplied joint mismatch probability Δ and requires matching block marginals; it neither proves a mixing rate nor supplies Δ. A continuously fitted readout, unbounded quadratic loss, reused training sample or data-dependent candidate construction needs its own complexity, tail and dependence controls. In-sample inference can instead target the realized panel with appropriately scoped dependence-aware intervals; cross-fitting is a possible generated-regressor/selection-bias tool, not a requirement to forecast.

Exposure similarity, common-factor covariance and residual spatial dependence are distinct targets. For SAR, factor loadings and innovations are propagated by P; for SEM the systematic factor exposure and residual multiplier differ; for SLX effects enter through the lagged covariates and there is no automatic SAR residual multiplier. Derive each restriction from its own specification. Bounded P and bounded V can support diversification only together with a sequence of well-diversified portfolios and uniform bounds (e.g. ‖w_N‖₂→0); neither establishes factor prices nor absence of asymptotic arbitrage. An APT pricing conclusion additionally requires a maintained approximate factor structure and residual-risk diversification, appropriate traded assets/factor portfolios or a separately stated spanning assumption, admissible portfolios and a no-asymptotic-arbitrage condition, plus any relevant factor moment and limiting assumptions. Estimate/test the resulting restrictions with uncertainty; an in-sample residual-covariance fit is evidence about dependence, not a test by itself of no-arbitrage or pricing. Do not infer identification of causal transmission from similarity links.

Diagnostics to request: (1) row margins, ties and independent simultaneous readout/proxy-error bounds; (2) raw adjacency symmetry, degree range, isolates, row sums, λ boundary distance and sensitivity to graph perturbations; (3) innovation eigenvalues, fitted parameter inventory and model-specific covariance; (4) cross-sectional/time dependence and which fitted stages each interval refits; (5) explicit factors, traded/spanning assumptions, pricing errors and diversification sequence. These diagnose premises; finite-sample checks do not prove the asymptotic economic assumptions.
