\section{Conclusion}

A financial peer comparison depends on the benchmark and the information
both methods can see. After adjustment for FF5 and momentum, TabPFN peers
based on four ratios improve in-sample return fit over peers selected by
Fern\'andez's joint distance. Under market controls, selecting peers directly
from those same ratios accounts for much of the fit. With broader financial
disclosures, TabPFN delivers the largest estimated gain over its own
direct-input benchmark among the twelve combinations examined.

The geometry of that representation helps explain what the comparison
measures. Its leading coordinate orders firms strongly by valuation, yet
projecting firms onto that coordinate changes their neighbours and lowers
spatial fit. For an analyst using peer returns, the relevant evidence is
therefore how firms compare locally in the full representation. Our
counterexample shows why recovering a financial target alone cannot settle
that question, and the covariance bound identifies how the spatial
coefficient and the distribution of peer weights govern its consequences.
