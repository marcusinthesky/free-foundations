\section{Conclusion}

Frozen features offer a candidate measure of financial proximity.
Exact recoverability identifies which histories can share a
representation for a given target. It leaves room for recodings that
change their neighbours. The graph's normalization, degree ratio, and
spatial parameter then govern the sensitivity of a spatial covariance
calculation.

In the US characteristic reimplementation, the literal joint kernel
yields a large no-intercept estimate and an unavailable fit when the
intercept is included. The dated frozen-state exhibit describes an
embedding geometry visibly aligned with valuation inputs. A stronger
economic interpretation requires independent targets and tests on
matched samples. This is the distinction between a feature map that
retains financial information and a spatial network that measures a
particular economic relation.
