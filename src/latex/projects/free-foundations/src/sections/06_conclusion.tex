\section{Conclusion}

Spatial arbitrage pricing theory takes a measure of proximity between firms
as given, and our results show that the frozen internal representations of
TFMs can supply one, although what they add depends on the benchmark
against which they are judged. Using four financial ratios, every TFM we
study has a higher estimated fit than Fern\'andez's characteristic-history
distance, yet a direct distance on the same standardized ratios improves
on that distance by a comparable amount, so the improvement does not
require an encoder. Once broad filing data are added, by contrast, all four
TFMs have positive estimated gains over the direct distance, and the
largest survives five Fama--French factors and momentum.

Probing these representations shows what they have learned about firms.
In the ratio-only embedding, the leading coordinates explain almost all of
the rank variation in valuation and almost none of the variation in market
beta, and the selected broad-input representation is organized along the
same valuation axis. That dominant axis does not determine the peers,
however. Firms close on it have few neighbours in common with the full
representation, and selecting peers along it lowers fit by more than the
model's gain over the direct distance. Omitting the valuation history
changes peers substantially, yet its interval for the change in fit
excludes a loss of that size. Read alongside
Proposition~\ref{prop:recovery}, these results show that neither
prediction accuracy, nor return fit, nor the leading axis identifies the
peers a representation selects.

An encoder proposed as the proximity measure for S-APT should therefore be
compared with a direct distance on its own inputs, and its peers should be
read from the full representation rather than from its dominant
coordinate. Because our comparisons are in-sample and retrospective, the
natural next step is held-out evaluation at each formation date, using the
model releases and filing values then available. Such an evaluation would
also allow a fair comparison with representations trained on financial
data, whose capacity held-out performance can discipline
\citep{kelly2024virtue}.
