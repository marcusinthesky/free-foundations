\section{Conclusion}

For US equities over 2011--2025, what a frozen encoder adds to peer
selection depends on the benchmark against which it is judged. Although
peers chosen by TabPFN from four ratios fit contemporaneous returns better
than peers chosen by Fern\'andez's characteristic-history distance, and the
difference survives five Fama--French factors and momentum, a ten-neighbour
graph on the same standardized ratios fits at least as well as TabPFN under
market controls, so that improvement does not require an encoder. Against
the direct graph, by contrast, encoding adds fit once the inputs extend to
broad filing data, where all four encoders have positive estimated gains
and the TabPFN gain persists under multifactor controls.

While the selected broad-input representation is organized along a
valuation axis, its peers are not. Firms close on that axis have few
neighbours in common with the full representation, and selecting peers
along it lowers fit by more than the encoder's gain over the direct graph,
whereas omitting the valuation history changes peers substantially without
a detectable change in fit. Read alongside
Proposition~\ref{prop:recovery}, these results show that neither
prediction accuracy, nor return fit, nor the leading axis identifies the
peers a representation selects.

Because none of these summaries settles which firms an encoder treats as
peers, an encoder proposed for peer selection should be compared with a
direct distance on its own inputs, and its peers should be read from the
full representation rather than from its dominant coordinate. Since our
comparisons are in-sample and retrospective, the natural next test is
whether the broad-input gain carries over to peers formed with the model
releases and filing values available at each formation date.
