\section{Conclusion}

The training objective supplies an economic interpretation when its
predictions distinguish the exposures of interest. In the ratio-only
application the leading coordinates track valuation closely and show
little alignment with estimated market beta. The return comparisons
evaluate a different use of the representation, selecting peers whose
returns enter a factor-adjusted spatial model.

For an analyst constructing a peer return from financial statements, the
value of a frozen representation depends on the information available for
the comparison. With the four characteristic histories used by
Fern\'andez, TabPFN selects peers whose returns improve in-sample fit over
her joint distance after adjustment for FF5 and momentum. Against the more
direct benchmark of firms close in standardized feature space, the clearest
gain for TabPFN comes when both methods use the broader financial disclosures
and the return model controls for the market.

Within that broad representation, valuation gives the leading coordinate a
recognizable economic interpretation. Firms with similar values on this
axis, however, often occupy different neighbourhoods in the full space.
Restricting peer selection to the valuation coordinate consequently changes
whose returns enter each firm's peer average, and the resulting graph fits
the quarterly returns less well. For spatial asset pricing, this local
ordering matters in its own right, since even representations that recover
the same exposure can assign different peer weights and imply different
covariances.
