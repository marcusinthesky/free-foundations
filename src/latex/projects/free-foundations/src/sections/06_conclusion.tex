\section{Conclusion}

An analyst selecting peer firms must justify the distance used to
rank them, because even an encoding that retains the desired exposure
can change which firms are closest.
The graph's normalization, degree ratio, and
spatial parameter then govern the sensitivity of a spatial covariance
calculation.

In the US characteristic reimplementation, the literal joint kernel
yields a large no-intercept estimate and a fit flagged as invalid or
on the boundary when the
intercept is included. On the quarterly panel, the estimated gain
from encoding depends on
the reference graph. Using direct financial inputs as the reference
leaves the incremental lag fit uncertain. The dated exhibit describes an
embedding geometry visibly aligned with valuation inputs. Comparing
the same firms on independently measured exposures would establish
how closely the selected peers resemble each other on a quantity
outside the encoder inputs. This is the distinction between a feature map that
retains financial information and a spatial network that measures a
particular economic relation.
