# Academic manuscript

## Workflow

Start at the requested stage. For a new section, work from outline to draft. For an existing manuscript, begin with the passage in reader order. A review produces findings; an annotation request places them in the source. Match the extent of a revision to the author's request.

1. **Establish the reader.** Identify the venue, the economic or scientific question, the closest comparison and the answer the evidence supports. Read the surrounding text and a comparable paper when available. Name one practice the audience can reasonably take for granted and one paper-specific concept they need to learn. For a mixed audience, find the point where each group needs the other's expertise. Record assumptions about the readership where venue guidance is unavailable.
2. **Tape out the section.** Use the [section outline](assets/section-outline.md) for a planning deliverable. Write one substantive bullet per paragraph. Attach the specific proof, observation, exhibit or citation that earns the claim. Mark a proposed result as a hypothesis until evidence exists. Read the bullets in order. Locate the first missing premise, duplicated conclusion or change of subject. If two analyses have separate purposes, introduce the second on its own terms.
3. **Score the outline.** Decide where to prepare a concept, slow down for the mechanism, or let a finding carry the emphasis. Expand each difficult bullet with the detail the reader needs there and the reason the next paragraph follows. If using a reference paper as a model, identify the rhetorical move and apply it to the author's own evidence. The [worked score](references/paragraph-score.md) follows one change of sample through to finished prose.
4. **Draft.** Establish the economic object before introducing notation readers must interpret. Keep a familiar subject across sentences when that helps the reader follow it. Give the principal finding a main clause. Use a conjunction when you can name the condition, cause, concession or comparison it expresses. Preserve the author's terminology, formal assumptions, sampling units, uncertainty, citations and generated values. Consult [literature and theory](references/literature-and-theory.md) for positioning and proofs, or [empirical exposition](references/empirical-exposition.md) for data and exhibits.
5. **Read cold, then edit.** Set aside the outline and earlier review labels. Sketch what a reader actually learns from each paragraph. Compare that sequence with the intended contribution and trace the most consequential mismatch to a passage. Use the [editorial review](references/editorial-review.md) to examine local transitions, rhetorical questions, cadence and repeated caveats. Re-read edited passages with their neighbours. Give priority to obstacles that change how a reader understands the result.
6. **Hand off in the requested form.** For each finding give the location, reader difficulty, smallest useful change and meaning to preserve. For LaTeX annotation, insert standalone `% REVIEW:` comment lines and prefix each continuation with `%`. Compare with the starting working copy to confirm that removing just the inserted lines recovers its bytes, including existing comments and local edits. For an optional second look at surface patterns, run `just latex::prose-lint` from the repository root in devenv. Read each match in context and keep effective prose when the wording earns its place.
