# Literature and theory

## Position a contribution

Give each literature paragraph a substantive question. Identify the closest comparison and read its actual result. Compare the papers on an axis that changes the inference, such as their target, identifying assumptions, measurement, population or horizon. Explain what a reader who knows the earlier result can learn from this paper. When two designs estimate different quantities, name each before discussing an apparent disagreement.

## Explain a formal result

Introduce the economic object and the question the display resolves. Identify definitions and assumptions before using them in an implication. After the display, explain which assumption supplies the important step and what changes when it is relaxed. Preserve quantifiers, conditioning, the direction of implications, and distinctions between local and global results.

For example, an invertible recoding can preserve target recovery and change a nearest neighbour. This establishes a possibility. An empirical claim about a particular embedding needs evidence about that embedding. When moving from the counterexample to a covariance bound, explain why a change in neighbours can matter to a model that uses peer weights.
