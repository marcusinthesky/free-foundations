# Empirical exposition

## Data and design

Follow one unit from the source population into the analysis. Identify the date when the unit enters, the observations it contributes, the denominator used, and the restriction that changes eligibility. Distinguish security, issuer and issuer-quarter where the unit changes. Explain which variation answers the question. Describe missingness where it changes the represented population or the comparison.

## Tables and figures

Begin with what was compared. Give the estimate, units, relevant baseline, and the uncertainty the procedure measures. Tie a diagnostic to the specific inference it qualifies. For example, when the intercept fit is unavailable because its diagnostic reports an invalid or boundary fit, explain what the table cell means before interpreting the available estimate. Conditional refit percentiles describe variation across draws; a coverage interpretation needs additional support.

For a figure, identify what is directly plotted and what interpretation requires a model. When coordinate orientation is arbitrary, explain a ranking by absolute association in those terms. Name model inputs that reappear in an exhibit so the reader can judge the scope of the finding. End with the economic implication the evidence supports.
