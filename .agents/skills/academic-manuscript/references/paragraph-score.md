# Score a change of sample

A finance reader can follow return-based eligibility but may assume every exhibit uses the fitted sample. An ML reader may recognize a principal coordinate yet need to know why subsequent returns determined eligibility in the preceding fit. Both need the reason the roster changes.

## Tape out the join

- The spatial fit uses issuer portfolios with sufficient observed quarters and a converged first-stage fit. Anchor this claim in the sample construction and fitted-issuer count.
- The January geometry calculation uses the full analytical formation roster because its inputs are available at formation. Anchor this claim in the roster and the date of the states. Pause here to distinguish the roster for the coordinates from the observed subset for each ratio.
- Valuation ratios align more strongly in absolute terms with the leading coordinate than dividend yield or debt maturity. Give that finding the main clause. Explain its descriptive scope where readers first interpret it: the ratios entered the encoder.

## Flesh out and draft

The handoff belongs at the opening of the geometry paragraph. The return fit supplies familiar information, the change in question supplies the reason for a different roster, and the comparison of ratios supplies the finding.

> The return fit requires subsequent quarterly observations. Because the January embedding describes information available at formation, we compute its principal coordinates for the full analytical roster. For each ratio, we compare issuers with an observed value. The leading coordinate aligns most strongly in absolute rank correlation with the two valuation ratios, both of which enter the encoder.

The first sentence can be omitted if the preceding paragraph has just established eligibility. A connector such as *therefore* between the fit diagnostic and the valuation finding would imply an inference that neither exhibit tests.
